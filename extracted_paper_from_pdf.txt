Abstract
Quantum computers running Shor’s algorithm threaten cloud stor-
age by breaking RSA/ECC public-key cryptography, enabling Har-
vest Now, Decrypt Later (HNDL) attacks. Direct deployment of
post-quantum primitives like NIST ML-KEM (CRYSTALS-Kyber)
introduces key expansion (12.5×), WAN fragmentation, and cen-
tralized key exposure. We design, formally verify, and evaluate a
multi-layered quantum-resistant hybrid cloud storage architecture
synthesizing five defenses: (1) an Adaptive Encryption Decision
Engine (AEDE) matching cipher strength to payload sensitivity and
size; (2) a Dual-Key Hybrid KEM combining SECP256R1 and Kyber-
512 via HKDF-SHA256 ensuring classical-quantum IND-CCA2 se-
curity; (3) Zero-Trust Shamir (2,3)-threshold sharing over GF(257)
eliminating single-point repository exposure; (4) an Early-Drop
outer HMAC-SHA256 boundary filter dropping forged requests
in 0.089 ms (12.3× speedup against DoS); and (5) an append-only
SHA-256 hash-chain audit ledger. Symbolic verification in ProVerif
2.05 proves master key secrecy. Xeon benchmarks show hybrid en-
cryption incurs only 1.28 ms overhead for 1 MB payloads (0.16 ms
delta over ECC), with 79% AVX-512 energy reduction.

1 Introduction
Enterprise cloud storage relies on public-key ciphers (RSA-3072,
ECC-256) [3, 8] secured by integer factorization (IFP) and elliptic
curve discrete logarithms (ECDLP) [6]. While classical algorithms
require sub-exponentialO(exp((𝑐+𝑜( 1))(ln𝑛) 1/3(ln ln𝑛) 2/3)) or
exponentialO(√𝑝) time, Shor’s algorithm [12] breaks both in poly-
nomial timeO(𝑛 3). A∼4,000-qubit quantum device compromises
RSA/ECC in seconds, empowering adversaries to execute “Har-
vest Now, Decrypt Later” (HNDL) attacks on 50-year regulatory
archives such as HIPAA and financial records [ 9]. Naive deploy-
ment of NIST ML-KEM (CRYSTALS-Kyber) [1, 4] introduces three
critical operational barriers:
(1) Key Expansion Overhead:Kyber-512 public keys require
800 bytes, representing a12.5× expansion over SECP256R1
(65 bytes).
(2) W AN MTU Fragmentation:Combined lattice public keys
and ciphertexts exceed standard 1500-byte WAN MTUs,
resulting in packet fragmentation and connection latency
penalties.
(3) Central Repository Exposure:Centralised key manage-
ment services (KMS) are a single-point target for cata-
strophic exposure and exfiltration of databases [10].
Contributions.We provide the following five key technical con-
tributions:
(1) Holistic Multi-Layered Quantum-Resistant Cloud Stor-
age Architecture:An architecture that combines dynamic
client profiling with a server-side zero-trust repository.
(2) IND-CCA2 Hybrid Key Encapsulation Mechanism
(KEM):A hybrid key establishment mechanism based on
SECP256R1 ECDH and Kyber-512, with HKDF-SHA256 for
secure key derivation, designed to protect against classical
and quantum adversaries.
(3) Zero-Trust Shamir Secret Sharing:A secret-sharing scheme
over the Galois field GF(257), designed to provide zero Shan-
non mutual information between the secret and an individ-
ual repository share.
(4) Early-Drop HMAC-SHA256 Boundary Filter:An outer
authentication filter that rejects forged payloads in 0.089 ms,
achieving a reported12.3× speedup compared with CPU-
exhaustion DoS floods.
(5) Formal Verification and Performance Evaluation:For-
mal verification using ProVerif 2.05 to analyze master-key
secrecy and boundary invariance, complemented by bare-
metal Xeon microbenchmarks for payloads ranging from
100 KB to 10 MB.

3.4 Early-Drop Outer HMAC Boundary
Verification
Post-quantum decapsulation requires significant CPU compute
(1.095ms in unoptimized pipelines). Attackers could forge invalid
ciphertexts to cause CPU saturation. To neutralize this vector, the
client signs fixed-length container headers using an outer HMAC-
SHA256 key 𝐾𝑔𝑤 pre-shared during tenant onboarding (Table 2,
Algorithm 1).
3.5 Forensic Hash-Chain Audit Ledger
All storage lifecycle transactions are appended to an immutable,

7 Conclusion
We designed, formally verified, and empirically evaluated a quantum-
resistant adaptive hybrid cloud storage architecture. By synthesiz-
ing AEDE dynamic profiling, dual-key hybrid KEM (SECP256R1
+ Kyber-512), zero-trust Shamir (2,3)-sharing over GF(257), early-
drop outer HMAC filtering, and an immutable SHA-256 audit ledger,
our system eliminates single-point repository exposure and HNDL
vulnerabilities. ProVerif 2.05 confirms master key secrecy and early-
drop invariance. Bare-metal Xeon benchmarks demonstrate that hy-
brid KEM introduces only 0.16 ms overhead for 1 MB payloads, AVX-
512 vectorization reduces energy by 79%, and early-drop HMAC
stops DoS decapsulation storms in 0.089 ms (12.3×speedup).
